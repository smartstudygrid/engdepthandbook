3. Curriculum & Teaching Expectations Section 3.0

3.1 Five Core Curriculum Strands

Whatever the external examination specification (Cambridge IGCSE or Common Core), the department's curriculum is organised around five strands that recur in every grade with increasing cognitive demand:

1. Reading Literature

Poetry, drama, and prose close reading; understanding form, context, and literary devices.

2. Non-Fiction & Media

Rhetoric, bias, source reliability, argumentative structure, and multi-modal digital texts.

3. Process Writing

Analytical, transactional, and creative prose; planning, drafting, peer-editing, and revising.

4. Speaking & Listening

Formal presentations, structured debates, active listening, and academic discussion protocols.

5. Language & Vocab

Explicit tier-2/tier-3 academic vocabulary instruction, grammar, and syntax in context.

3.2 Non-Negotiable Lesson Expectations

  • Learning Objectives & Success Criteria: Shared visibly and referred to throughout the lesson, not merely stated at the beginning.
  • Active Student Engagement: Students read, write, or speak at length. A lesson in which students only listen or copy is incomplete.
  • Full-Sentence Answers & Cold Calling: Teachers insist on academic vocabulary and full-sentence responses.
  • Process Writing & Draft Revisions: Writing is taught as a process. Students receive feedback on drafts and redraft prior to final grading.
  • Generative AI Policy: Generative AI may be used for brainstorming or revision only when explicitly authorized by the teacher. Uncredited AI generation submitted as original student work constitutes academic dishonesty.

3.3 Differentiated Learning Support Protocols

Special Educational Needs (SEN / IEP)
  • Review student Individual Education Plans (IEPs) at start of term.
  • Provide access arrangements: extra reading/writing time, enlarged text, graphic organizers.
  • Reduce task load (fewer items) rather than lowering intellectual rigor.
Gifted & Talented (G&T)
  • Stretch through depth, cognitive complexity, and independent research.
  • Avoid assigning extra volume of repetitive work or using solely as peer tutors.
  • Encourage critical theory evaluation and literary competition entries.
English as an Additional Language (EAL)
  • Pre-teach key academic vocabulary and supply bilingual glossaries.
  • Allow scaffolded sentence stems and first-language outline planning.
  • Separate language mechanics demand from conceptual understanding during initial feedback.

4.2 Diagnostic Baseline Assessments

Administered in Weeks 2–3 (Aug 30 – Sep 6, 2026) across all grades to diagnose student gaps in reading comprehension, grammar, and written conventions.

  • Duration: 20–30 minutes (approx. 20 MCQs and short constructed responses).
  • Grading Impact: Diagnostic only; entered in LMS/Schoology to track learning gaps but does not alter the summative quarter score.
  • Uniformity: Standardized test paper across both Boys' and Girls' sections.

5.2 End-of-Semester Exam Formatting

  • Grades 4–12 Main Paper: 40 Marks (2 Hours duration).
  • Grades 4–12 Writing Paper: 10 Marks (40 Minutes duration).
  • Grade 3 English Paper: 20 Marks (2 Hours duration).
  • Font & Layout: Arial font only (16pt Main Questions, 14pt Sub-questions). Footer format: Page X of Y.
  • Answer Space: Ample lined writing space provided directly on question papers.

5.3 Official Marking Color Protocol

Red Pen: Subject Teacher (Initial marking & sub-totals).
Black Pen: Moderator / Checker (Audit verification).
Green Pen: Subject Director (Final 5% sample audit).

7. Professional Learning & Operations Section 7.0

7.1 Communities of Practice (CoP) & CFAs

Teachers participate in fortnightly grade-level CoP sessions centered on student work artifacts and Common Formative Assessment (CFA) data analysis.

  • Agreed termly focus on specific skill inquiry (e.g., integrating textual evidence).
  • Collaborative moderation of student essay samples prior to final grading.
  • Reteaching protocols agreed for identified skill deficits.

7.2 Non-Evaluative Coaching Cycle

Instructional coaching in the English Department is developmental, non-appraisal based, and confidential.

1. Goal Setting → 2. Baseline → 3. Co-Plan/Model → 4. Practice → 5. Action Feedback

7.3 Parent & Official Communication Channels

Important Rule: All communications with parents must occur exclusively through official school channels (Schoology, MyAIS, or formal school email). Direct telephone calls or sharing personal mobile numbers with parents is strictly prohibited.
